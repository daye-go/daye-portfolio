V-pos Karaoke Operations POS System Design

DIAGNOSIS

V-pos is a POS system whose screens and features can be customized by store type. In this project, I rebuilt a restaurant-based POS into a karaoke operations flow where staff could manage rooms, time, drink orders, reservations, sales, and schedules from one device.

The existing POS followed a restaurant model: choose menu items, send orders, and take payment. Karaoke works differently. Staff first check room status, open a room, set the session time, add drink orders during use, and combine the time fee and order total at checkout.

The problem was not that a few karaoke features were missing. The product was trying to run a time-based business with a menu-based structure. So I defined the core challenge as rebuilding the information architecture around rooms and time, while making the first shift understandable without long training.

APPROACH

The design direction was simple: match the POS structure to the way karaoke staff actually work. The first screen had to read like a room map, frequent actions had to stay visible, and time, orders, reservations, and payment had to remain connected to the same room.

Room status → session time → drink order → reservation check → payment → sales and schedule check.
This became the operating sequence for the product. The goal was not to add more functions, but to make the next action obvious from the screen.

DESIGN 01
Room time & drink orders

I changed the POS starting point from menu selection to room and time.

Time selection and remaining time
I designed the time-management flow the restaurant POS did not have. When staff tap a room, they can choose the session length and start the clock. Once the session begins, the remaining time counts down live on the room screen.

I placed room status at the center of the interface. Instead of reading a list, staff can understand which rooms are empty, occupied, or nearly finished through a layout that mirrors the physical venue.

Extensions, early closes, and drink orders
Karaoke sessions change often. Guests extend time, leave early, or add drinks mid-session. I kept these actions inside the selected room context so staff did not have to leave the flow and reinterpret the room state.

“Good karaoke POS UX should not make staff memorize how to use it. The room status should naturally reveal what to do next.”

Drink orders are added while the room is already selected. This binds time and orders to the same room, so checkout can combine the session fee and drink total without manual calculation.

DESIGN 02
Reservations · Sales · Schedules

I moved the information staff checked every day into the POS they already used.

Reservation management
Reservations were often taken by phone and recorded on paper or in messenger threads. When the person on duty changed, details could leak, and duplicate bookings could happen. I moved room, time, and party size into the POS so reservations appeared where operations actually happened.

Reservations and current occupancy share the same context. Staff can see not only which room is free now, but also which room has an upcoming reservation before assigning guests.

Sales and schedule access
Sales and shift schedules were information staff checked every day, but they lived outside the POS. I brought them into the device staff already used throughout the shift, reducing the number of places they had to check.

The final delivery connected room layout, session time, remaining time, extensions, early closes, drink orders, reservations, sales, and schedules into one operating flow. The karaoke version of V-pos became a POS organized around rooms and time, not menus.

LEARNING

Changing the business type was not a matter of swapping screens. A restaurant POS rests on the premise that the store sells menu items; a karaoke POS rests on the premise that the store sells time. When the premise changes, the information structure has to change with it.

Reducing training burden did not mean reducing functionality. By placing frequent actions on the first screen and letting the layout explain sequence, the product could keep the full operating flow while helping new staff choose the next action faster.

What I would validate next
The project was led through launch, but it did not close with quantitative measurement. For the next step, I would track first-shift training time, time to first completed payment, duplicate reservation incidents, and sales or schedule questions before and after release.

When what the business sells changes, the screen must change what it makes obvious.
