Redefining the Financial Journey: MOMENT SILO

"No one should lose out simply because finance is hard."
We rewrite every financial moment into the "simplest experience in the world."

While other silos at Toss focus on being the best-performing "trees" in their respective domains, the MOMENT SILO is the squad that designs the entire "forest." We don’t get buried in specific features; instead, we move with a bold ambition: to take full responsibility for every single moment a user encounters finance in their daily life.

From Product-Centric Functions to User Mental Models
We refuse to think in industrial, functional units like "Remittance," "Payments," or "Insurance." Instead, we obsess over the fundamental questions users face every day: "Is this transaction safe?" "What if I’m short on cash right now?" "Am I missing out on benefits I’m entitled to?"

Bridging the Experience Gaps through Collaboration: MOMENT SILO overlooks the entire platform’s user experience, identifying the "experience voids" and backlogs that often fall between different silos.

Cross-Silo Orchestration: To ensure a seamless organic flow that specific functional teams might overlook, we collaborate intensely with various squads. We integrate fragmented features into one cohesive, unified "Financial Journey."

The Role of a Designer Who Sees the Forest
As a Product Designer in the MOMENT SILO, I’ve spent my time proposing the most intuitive and effortless solutions at every crossroads of a user's financial life. I translate complex, provider-defined jargon into the user's natural language. By orchestrating the technical strengths of various squads, I build products that proactively suggest the financial actions users actually intended.

Finance is no longer a "homework assignment" you have to study for. With Toss, it becomes an effortless, natural part of everyday life. At MOMENT SILO, we are redrawing the map of finance so that every user’s moment flows without interruption.

My Role: As the DRI of the Moment Silo, I designed the end-to-end financial journey rather than individual features.

As the DRI designer for the Moment Silo, my role extended far beyond improving the usability of a single feature. I was responsible for designing not only the individual moments in which users interact with financial services, but also the broader flow that connects those moments into one cohesive journey.

In close collaboration with 15 cross-functional squads, I uncovered overlooked user needs and VOC across services such as money transfer, cards, and insurance, areas where business priorities and feature-based structures often left meaningful pain points unresolved. I translated those findings into tangible experience improvements across the product.

My role was not to refine each functional “tree” in isolation, but to step back and shape the entire “forest” of the experience, rebuilding Toss from the user’s perspective as a more connected and intuitive financial journey.

Leading collaboration across product, engineering, and research, I took ownership of the full end-to-end design process, from problem definition and UX strategy to execution and iteration.

Rationale
Evidence-Based Design

Why wasn’t Toss present in the moments when users found finance most difficult?

Traditional usability testing can uncover friction inside a product, but it cannot fully explain the real-world moments when users struggle with finance and the product is absent altogether.

We realized that we had been looking at convenience from a provider-led perspective, optimizing functional ease without deeply understanding the lived reality of users.

As the sole designer in the Moment Silo, I partnered with the research team to create a direct VOC channel through which users could share their financial frustrations in their own language. This was not designed as a simple feedback form. Rather, it was a mechanism for capturing the moments when users’ financial lives broke down, beyond product features, beyond interface-level friction.

By collecting raw, unfiltered user voices, we gained access to needs that had previously been obscured by feature- and business-driven thinking. This allowed us not only to uncover what users truly needed, but also to translate those needs into meaningful product opportunities. At the same time, the initiative served a branding function: it gave users the sense that they were not just consuming Toss, but actively participating in shaping it.

The insight was decisive: users do not think in features. They think in situations, limitations, emotions, and moments of frustration. These qualitative signals became a critical foundation for product direction and the strongest evidence for advocating user-centered priorities across the organization.

Ultimately, this domain around moments of financial need became more than a research effort. It functioned as research infrastructure, an internal persuasion tool, and a brand experience that made Toss’s customer-centricity visible and believable.

Bridging the Gap: Cardless ATM

Case Study 1. Solving a Physical Constraint in Finance with an MVP: Cardless ATM Withdrawal

Need cash unexpectedly, but left your wallet behind? You’re still okay.

Unexpected moments of financial friction happen to everyone. When users leave their card behind or go out without a wallet, they often find themselves in a frustrating situation: they have money in their account, but no practical way to access it.

Research showed us that users wanted a service that could solve this exact moment. To address that need, we designed an end-to-end cardless ATM withdrawal flow that enabled users to withdraw cash using only their smartphone.

This was not simply about adding convenience. Despite complex security requirements and technical constraints, we focused on the user’s real goal: getting cash immediately and with confidence. Through intensive cross-functional collaboration, we developed a secure and intuitive flow that ultimately launched as a real product, turning a common everyday inconvenience into a solvable moment.

At Toss, MVPs like this are not just feature experiments. They are fast, practical ways to remove friction from users’ financial lives and give them back a sense of control and freedom.

Representative Problems Solved by the Moment Silo

1. Transfer Maintenance Response (Problem Solve 01) - Solving the Golden Time of Transfers with an MVP
Have you ever urgently needed to send money, only to be blocked by bank maintenance hours?

Daily bank maintenance windows may be brief, but for users who need to transfer money at that exact moment, the frustration is significant. We defined this not as a simple system maintenance issue, but as a failure experience in missing the golden time for a transfer.

Instead of pursuing a complex system overhaul, we designed a single bottom sheet MVP using the design system. At the exact moment users felt stuck, it clearly communicated the maintenance status and immediately suggested the next best action. By focusing not on building another feature, but on the user’s core goal of sending money when it matters most, we were able to quickly relieve one of the most frequent and painful moments of frustration.

2. Preventing Credit Card Delinquency (Problem Solve 03) - Defining Mistakes as an Experience Problem, Not a Personal Failure
Have you ever missed your card payment by just a moment, only to see your credit score affected?

Even in a world full of convenient financial apps, many users still miss card payments in the middle of busy daily lives and end up facing unnecessary risk. We saw this not as carelessness on the user’s part, but as a failure of financial information to connect with the context of everyday life.

Rather than stopping at a simple “upcoming payment” alert, we focused on designing information that helped users recognize what they were about to miss, and why it mattered, at the right moment. By restructuring payment timing and credit impact around the user’s mental model, we enabled users to go beyond merely using a feature and instead feel a greater sense of control over their financial state.

3. Responding to Voice Phishing Anxiety (Problem Solve 05) - Designing a Psychological Safety Net Before Financial Action
Have you ever received a message from an unknown number and wondered whether the transfer might be part of a scam?

Voice phishing schemes continue to grow more sophisticated, and financial anxiety remains widespread. We focused on the split second of doubt users experience just before tapping the transfer button. A generic warning message saying “Be careful” was never going to be enough.

We translated risk signals into contextual guidance that users could understand intuitively. By designing a flow that helped users pause, assess the situation, and make a safer decision for themselves, we positioned Toss not simply as a money transfer tool, but as the most trustworthy financial guide in moments of uncertainty.

4. Insurance Claims and Coverage Check (Problem Solve 07) - Turning Forgotten Insurance into an Actionable Experience
Have you ever paid for insurance every month, but felt lost about what support you could actually receive when you got sick?

For most people, insurance disappears from everyday life once they sign up. But when coverage is actually needed, complex policy language and difficult procedures often prevent users from taking action. This is not just a product problem; it is a breakdown in experience.

We reframed insurance not as a difficult financial product, but as a tool for resolving risks in everyday life. By translating coverage information into language users could easily understand and increasing the density of the experience so that action could happen immediately when needed, we pushed against a provider-centered insurance market and continued testing what it means to truly put user goals first.

Meaningful Quality, Proven by Scale

After launch, these features became some of the most positively received outcomes in the history of the Moment Silo. At the scale of 19.1 million MAU and an average of 270 app sessions per month, even the smallest difference in quality translated directly into user reassurance, and ultimately into business value.

This was never just about creating polished screens. By leaving behind a framework of Listen → Validate → Solve within the organization, we gave Toss a more complete visual and logical foundation for how it contributes to users’ everyday lives.
